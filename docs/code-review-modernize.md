- **Inline picker:** the Select tab shows the system photo picker in the page instead of a sheet. Tapping Edit or Export before photos are picked makes the Select tab shake its head, with the error haptic (just the haptic under Reduce Motion), and VoiceOver announces "Select some photos first"; this replaced a "Select some photos first!" line below the picker. This was found by trying settings on the iPhone, since Apple doesn't document it. - **Blurred photo fill:** a drop button next to the color well fills the border with the photo itself, scaled to cover the canvas and blurred, instead of the plain color. It's off by default. While it's on, a blur-strength slider (shown as 5–100% in steps of 5, default 40%, where 100 is a blur of 10% of the canvas) takes the color well's place. Strength is `BorderSettings.blurFraction`, measured against the canvas's shorter side, so the preview and every export size look equally soft. The export blurs a 512px copy and scales it up, which keeps it cheap at any canvas size. Builds and passes its unit tests on the iOS 26.5 simulator; the drop button, 4% default and strength slider have been checked on the iPhone 16 Pro.
- **Separate border for each side:** a dashed-square button next to Border Width switches to four sliders, one along each edge of the preview, and the preview shrinks to make room. Each side starts at the current single width the first time, and keeps its value while the mode is off. The values are saved with the other settings and apply to the whole batch. Each side is a minimum gap and the photo is centered in the space left, so it's never cropped. The per-side widths are `BorderSettings.sides` (`BorderInsets`), switched on by `isPerSide`, and `layout` rounds each inset separately on the native-resolution path. Unit tests cover equal sides matching the single width and uneven sides keeping each width; on the native path the bottom gap can be up to 2px off from rounding.
- **Values in the slider thumb:** Border Width, Blur Strength and the edge sliders show their number inside the thumb. While dragging, it lifts onto a dark, thumb-sized pill and follows the thumb. It goes above the slider, below it for the bottom edge, and 84pt toward the photo for the left and right edges so it isn't hidden under your finger. The thumb's size is an estimate, since the system slider doesn't expose it.

Both were checked on the iPhone 16 Pro.

The details are the date taken, the title (IPTC or XMP title or caption), the filename, a handle (Instagram, Flickr, @, website or text only), the camera, focal length, aperture, shutter speed and ISO. A button at the start of each row spreads the row across the photo's width or groups it at the left, center or right. It's a share extension rather than an action extension because only share extensions get the full-color app icon; action extensions get a one-color template image. App Store Connect rejects app icons that have an alpha channel at all (ITMS-90717), and one developer reports that extension icons in the same bundle were checked too. Apple doesn't document whether action extension icons are. Archive the app and run Validate App in Xcode's Organizer to find out. If it fails, remove `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_APPICON_NAME` and the asset catalog from the `AddBorderAction` target; the list entry then gets iOS's generic extension icon, and the app-row entry keeps the full-color icon.

A summary lists the ratio, the border (on the Border Width slider's 0–64 scale, each side when they differ, with the color or "blurred photo"), the download size, and the number of photos with their combined file size, which is counted while the question shows. The summary stays below the progress bar while photos save. The Confirm Settings switch in the app's Settings tab turns it back on; the switch is the stored value inverted. The app's page in the Settings app now only shows the version (written to the App Group at launch) and the Acknowledgements.

- **HDR export (iOS 18+):** HDR photos keep their bright highlights in HEIC and JPEG exports (Download, Share, the Shortcuts action and the share-sheet entries), while the border, blurred fill, captions and shadow stay exactly as on an SDR screen. The canvas is still the photo's SDR rendition, drawn as before. Next to it, `ImageComposer.GainMap` writes the photo's own ISO 21496-1 gain map, turned upright and laid out like the canvas on a small canvas of its own (at most the gain map's resolution), clipped to the rounded corners, and filled everywhere else with the value that means "no boost". Its metadata is copied unchanged.
  - The earlier attempt let ImageIO derive a gain map from an HDR canvas (`kCGImageDestinationEncodeToISOGainmap`), which tone-maps the whole canvas and shifted the photo's exposure and the border color. Copying the photo's gain map leaves every SDR pixel untouched.
  - PNG, photos without an ISO gain map (including ones with only Apple's older gain map, whose brightness lives in the maker notes that exports remove), and iOS 17 export SDR as before.
  - A "Keep HDR" switch in the Settings tab's Saving group (iOS 18+, on by default) turns it off (`BorderSettings.keepsHDR`). Its footnote says when PNG rules HDR out. The share extension's "Saves" row lists HDR when it applies.
  - The Edit tab's preview shows the photo with its HDR highlights (`Thumbnail.hdrImage`, `.allowedDynamicRange(.high)`) whenever Download will keep them; the blurred fill stays SDR, as in the export.
  - An "HDR" badge in the top-right corner of each card (Select and Edit pages) marks photos with an ISO gain map. It's dimmed to "HDR off" when Keep HDR is off or the format is PNG, and VoiceOver says which. Whether Download keeps HDR is `BorderSettings.savesHDR`, which the preview, the badge, the Settings footnote and the share extension's summary all use. Checked on the iPhone 16 Pro.
  - The Simulator's ImageIO doesn't apply gain maps (it drops their metadata and decodes HDR as SDR), so there exports fall back to SDR and the HDR unit tests skip themselves; they run on a device. Their test photos, `multiborderTests/hdr-gradient*.jpg`, are made on a Mac by `scripts/make-hdr-fixtures.swift`, since the Simulator can't encode gain maps either.

Builds and passes its unit tests on the iOS 26.5 simulator (HDR tests skipped) and on the iPhone 16 Pro (iOS 27.0.1), where the HDR tests run: HEIC and JPEG keep the photo's highlights (above 2.5× SDR white) with the border at SDR white, the gain map turns upright with the photo, rounded corners cut it away, and it's dropped when off or saving PNG. A real iPhone 16 Pro HDR photo (4032×3024 HEIC, an ISO gain map of 2016×1512 with 2.59 stops of headroom) was also exported on the phone with a 0.1 border, as HEIC and JPEG, and compared with the original at a quarter size: the brightest highlight came out at 2.95× SDR white against the original's 2.97×, brightness differed from the original by 0.01 stops on average (HEIC; 0.02 for JPEG), the border stayed within 1% of SDR white on an HDR screen, and the SDR rendition matched the original. On the Mac, ImageIO doesn't return that photo's gain maps (at least inside Claude Code's sandbox), so checks with real photos need the phone. Exported photos in Photos, the preview's HDR and Share were then checked by eye on the iPhone 16 Pro, with the few HDR photos at hand; a wider range of photos (other cameras and apps, bright skies and lamps, portrait orientation) is still untested.

## Feature ideas

- **Clearing the picker's selection before the check mark:** the Clear tab only appears once the check mark hands photos over, because with `.default` selection (the only mode that keeps drag-to-select) the app can't see or change the picker's in-progress selection. Holding Clear while the picker showed and emptying the already-empty `selection` was tried and reverted before it was checked on the phone, since emptying an empty binding gives the picker nothing new to react to. Untried: giving the `PhotosPicker` a new `.id` on a hold, which should recreate it empty; the control would then have to show whenever the picker does, since the app can't tell whether anything is selected.
- **iPad and "Designed for iPad" on Mac:** the layout logic is already independent of resolution, so this is mostly UI work.
